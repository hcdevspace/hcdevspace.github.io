Motorsports has always been a sandbox for engineering and technological development. Teams and companies push the limits and look for ways to increase speed, efficiency, and reliability. One of the most influential developments in car racing history is the use of hybrid engines, which combine gasoline and electric power to produce higher performance and sustainability. From Formula 1 to endurance racing, hybrid engines have dramatically shaped motorsport to what it is now and have paved the path for an innovative future.

## What is a Hybrid Engine?
A hybrid engine combines a conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) with an electric motor or battery. Hybrid engines can either combine the power from both sources or switch between them to optimize efficiency and sustainability with an electric power source while also keeping the advantages of gasoline. Most hybrid cars operate the ICE alongside the electric motor to create more power and acceleration. Probably the most important feature of hybrids is regenerative braking. When the driver lifts off the accelerator, the electric motor reverses direction and converts the car's kinetic energy during deceleration to electrical energy which acts as a generator and charges the battery. The motor's resistance slows down the car. Regenerative braking keeps the electric battery charged and reduces the use of the normal brake pads, allowing for more efficient braking and longer life span for the brakes. These features and the benefits they bring are the reasons why hybrid engines are being actively used in motorsport.

## How Racecars Use Hybrid Engines' Benefits
Hybrid engines are actively being used in motorsport to actively increase power delivery, fuel efficiency, and environmental sustainability. Hybrids can employ their additional electric power to improve performance by amplifying horsepower with smaller engines and using the electric motor to enhance acceleration. Electric motors can provide peak **torque** instantly compared to combustion engines because they don't need time to "rev up" and rely on combustion cycles. Therefore, they can help the car launch immediately on acceleration, which supplies drivers with lots of lap time gains.

Since hybrid engines provide massive benefits and performance gains compared to tradition ICEs, many racing championships limit the amount of electrical energy drivers can use and deploy:
* In **F1**, cars use the MGU-K --- the electric motor system that uses regenerative braking --- to deploy additional electrical power for more horsepower, and this system is called ERS (Energy Recovery System). Strict ERS rules are present to limit the amount of electrical energy a driver can deploy per lap.
* In **endurance racing (World Endurance Championship, IMSA SportsCar Championship)**, the Hypercar/GTP classes use high performance hybrid powertrains not majorly for added power but mainly for increased fuel efficiency. Hybrid energy is limited by using a "virtual energy" system. Every car is limited to a certain amount virtual energy they can use per stint, and this amount includes both gasoline and electrical power used. Breaking this limit can lead to penalties.
* In **IndyCar**, there's no limit to how much additional electrical power you can deploy. However, the battery cannot hold unlimited power, so drivers still have to manage it strategically.

## Notable Hybrid Racecars
1. ### Mercedes-AMG Petronas F1 W11
   The W11 was one of the most dominant Formula 1 cars of all time. It combined a turbocharged V6 with an advanced ERS system that delivered insane power and acceleration and shattered many records and won the 2020 F1 championships.

1. ### Porsche 919 Hybrid
   The 919 competed in the WEC LMP1 class and was built for the toughest endurance races including the 24 Hours of Le Mans and other WEC races. It had a very complex hybrid electrical system combined with a V4 engine that brought excellent fuel efficiency, and this helped the Porsche 919 Hybrid achieve many race wins from 2015-2017.

Listening to the onboards of hybrid racecars, you can notice that the sound of the electric motor can have a very subtle volume or even as loud as the combustion engine itself. This highlights how different manufacturers and teams have implemented hybrid powertrains with distinct methods and approaches.

Overall, hybrid engines are reshaping motorsport, blending incredible performance with cutting-edge technology. As racing continues to evolve, these systems show how innovation, environmental sustainability, and speed can work together beautifully.
